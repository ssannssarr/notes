# ⚡ Electrochemistry - Complete Notes

---

## 📘 1. Introduction to Electrochemistry

**Electrochemistry** deals with the relationship between **electrical energy** and **chemical energy** — specifically, the study of redox reactions that involve this interconversion.

### Key Concepts:
- **Electrolysis** = Chemical energy is created from electrical energy (using electricity to cause a redox reaction)
- **Electrochemical Cell** = Electrical energy is created by converting chemical energy (a redox process produces electrical energy)

---

## 📗 2. Electrolytes

An **electrolyte** is a substance that conducts electricity when dissolved in water (or in molten state) by producing ions.

### Classification of Electrolytes

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Strong Electrolytes** | Completely dissociates into ions in water | NaCl, KCl, HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ |
| **Weak Electrolytes** | Partially dissociates/ionizes into ions | CH₃COOH, NH₄OH, NH₃, H₂CO₃ |
| **Non-Electrolytes** | Does not conduct electric current in molten state or aqueous solution | Urea, Glucose, Ethanol |

### Dissociation Example:
```
NaCl (aq) → Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)
H₂O → H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)
```

---

## 📙 3. Difference Between Electrolyte and Electrochemistry

| Property | Electrolyte | Electrochemistry |
|----------|-------------|------------------|
| **Definition** | A substance that conducts electricity when dissolved in water | A system/reaction involving the relationship between electrical and chemical energy |
| **Energy Conversion** | Chemical energy is created from electric energy | Electric energy is created by converting chemical energy |
| **Process Type** | Using electricity causes a redox reaction | A redox process produces electrical energy |
| **Focus** | The conducting medium itself | The entire system of electrical-chemical interconversion |

---

## 📕 4. Electrodes

**Electrodes** are metallic rods dipped into the electrolyte through which electricity enters or leaves the electrolyte.

### Two Types of Electrodes:

| Electrode | Type of Reaction | Charge | Function |
|-----------|------------------|--------|----------|
| **Anode** | Oxidation | Positive (+) | Electrons leave; loss of electrons |
| **Cathode** | Reduction | Negative (−) | Electrons enter; gain of electrons |

### 🔑 Memory Trick:
- **O**xidation = **L**oss of electrons (Anode)
- **R**eduction = **G**ain of electrons (Cathode)
- **"An Ox, Red Cat"** → Anode Oxidation, Reduction Cathode

---

## 📓 5. Electrolysis Reactions

### A) Electrolysis of Water (H₂O)

**At Cathode (Reduction):**
$\$2H^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2 \uparrow$$

**At Anode (Oxidation):**
$\$2OH^- \rightarrow H_2O + \tfrac{1}{2}O_2 + 2e^-$$

### B) Electrolysis of Aqueous NaCl Solution

**Dissociation:**
$$NaCl \rightarrow Na^+ + Cl^-$$

**At Cathode (Reduction):**
$$Na^+ + e^- \rightarrow Na \text{ (Sodium deposited at cathode)}$$

**At Anode (Oxidation):**
$\$2Cl^- \rightarrow Cl_2 \uparrow + 2e^- \text{ (Cl₂ gas released at anode)}$$

### C) Electrolysis of Molten NaCl

**Dissociation:**
$$NaCl (l) \rightarrow Na^+ + Cl^-$$

**At Cathode:**
$$Na^+ + e^- \rightarrow Na$$

**At Anode:**
$\$2Cl^- \rightarrow Cl_2 + 2e^-$$

## 📔 6. Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis

### 🔬 Faraday's First Law

**Statement:** The mass of a substance deposited or liberated at any electrode during electrolysis is **directly proportional** to the **quantity of electricity** passed through the electrolyte.

**Mathematical Form:**
$$W \propto Q$$
$$W = ZQ$$

Where:
- **W** = Mass of substance deposited (grams)
- **Q** = Quantity of electricity (Coulombs)
- **Z** = Electrochemical equivalent (g/C)

**Also:**
$$Q = I \times t$$

Where:
- **I** = Current (Amperes)
- **t** = Time (seconds)

**Relation:**
$$Z = \frac{W}{Q} = \frac{E}{F}$$

Where:
- **E** = Equivalent weight
- **F** = Faraday constant = 96,500 C

---

### 🔬 Faraday's Second Law

**Statement:** When the **same quantity of electricity** is passed through **different electrolytic solutions** connected in **series**, the ratio of the weights of different substances deposited at different electrodes is **equal to the ratio of their equivalent weights**.

**Mathematical Form:**
$$\frac{W_1}{W_2} = \frac{E_1}{E_2}$$

Where:
- **W₁, W₂** = Weights of substances deposited
- **E₁, E₂** = Equivalent weights of the substances

**Derivation:**
$$\because W_1 = Z_1 \times Q \quad \text{and} \quad W_2 = Z_2 \times Q$$

$$\therefore \frac{W_1}{W_2} = \frac{Z_1}{Z_2} = \frac{E_1}{E_2} \quad (\text{since } Z \propto E)$$

---

## 🔋 7. Faraday Constant (F)

**Definition:** The charge carried by **1 mole of electrons** is called **1 Faraday**.

### Calculation:

- Charge of 1 electron = \$1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ Coulombs
- 1 mole of electrons = \$6.022 \times 10^{23}$ electrons

**Charge of 1 mole of electrons:**
$$F = 6.022 \times 10^{23} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$$
$$F = 96{,}368 \text{ C} \approx 96{,}500 \text{ C}$$

$$\boxed{F = 96{,}500 \text{ Coulombs} = 1 \text{ Faraday}}$$

## ⚖️ 8. Equivalent Weight Calculations

### Formula:
$$\text{Equivalent Weight (E)} = \frac{\text{Atomic Weight (or Molecular Weight)}}{\text{Valency (n-factor)}}$$

### Examples:

| Compound | Atomic/Molecular Weight | Valency (n) | Equivalent Weight (E) |
|----------|------------------------|-------------|----------------------|
| **NaCl** | 23 (Na) | 1 | **23** |
| **Na⁺** | 23 | 1 | 23 |
| **Cu** | 64 | 2 | 32 |
| **Al** | 27 | 3 | 9 |
| **Ag** | 108 | 1 | 108 |
| **Mg(NO₃)₂** | 148 | 2 | 74 |
| **NaNO₃** | 85 | 1 | 85 |
| **BaSO₄** | 233 | 2 | 116.5 |
| **Fe₂SO₄** | 304 | 2 | 152 |
| **Zn(NO₃)₂** | 189 | 2 | 94.5 |
| **FeCl₃** | 162.5 | 3 | 54.17 |

## ✏️ 9. Numerical Problems

### 📌 Problem 1: Electrochemical Equivalent of Ag

**Question:** When 3 amperes of electric current is passed for 20 minutes through a AgNO₃ solution, 4 g of Ag is deposited. Calculate the electrochemical equivalent (Z) of Ag.

**Given:**
- Current (I) = 3 A
- Time (t) = 20 min = 1200 s
- Mass deposited (W) = 4 g

**Solution:**

Using Q = I × t:
$$Q = 3 \times 1200 = 3600 \text{ C}$$

Using Faraday's First Law: W = ZQ
$\$4 = Z \times 3600$$
$$Z = \frac{4}{3600} = \frac{1}{900} \text{ g/C}$$

$$\boxed{Z = 1.11 \times 10^{-3} \text{ g/C} \approx 0.001 \text{ g/C}}$$

---

### 📌 Problem 2: Mass of Silver Deposited

**Question:** What amount of silver (Ag) will be deposited when an electric current of **2.1 A** is passed through a **AgNO₃ solution** for **20 minutes**? (Atomic weight of Ag = 108 a.m.u.)

**Given:**
- Current (I) = 2.1 A
- Time (t) = 20 min = 1200 s
- Atomic weight of Ag = 108
- Valency = 1 → Equivalent weight (E) = 108
- Faraday constant (F) = 96,500 C

**Solution:**

Step 1: Calculate the charge (Q)
$$Q = I \times t = 2.1 \times 1200 = 2520 \text{ C}$$

Step 2: Use the formula
$$W = \frac{1}{F} \times E \times Q$$
$$W = \frac{1}{96500} \times 108 \times 2520$$
$$\boxed{W = 2.82 \text{ g of Ag}}$$

---

### 📌 Problem 3: Charge Required for Conversion

**Question:** How many coulombs of charge are required for the following conversion:
$$Cr^{2+}(aq) \rightarrow Cr(s) \text{ for 2 moles of electrons}$$

**Solution:**

The reaction is:
$$Cr^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow Cr(s)$$

For 2 moles of electrons, the charge required is:
$$Q = n \times F = 2 \times 96{,}500$$

$$\boxed{Q = 1{,}93{,}000 \text{ C} = 2 \text{ Faraday}}$$
