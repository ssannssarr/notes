    Detailed Study Notes: Fuels, Classification, and Combustion

    1. Definition of Fuel

    • Fuel: Any material that can react to release chemical or nuclear energy as heat or to be used to perform work.

    2. Classification of Fuels

    Fuels are broadly categorized based on their physical state, origin, and method of energy production.

    A. Solid Fuels

    • Definition: Solid materials burnt to produce energy and provide heating via combustion.
    • Examples: Coke, charcoal.

    B. Liquid Fuels

    • Definition: Combustible or energy-generating liquid molecules used to create mechanical/kinetic energy.
    • Key Characteristic: The fumes of liquid fuels are flammable, not the liquid fluid itself.
    • Origin: Most liquid fuels are derived from fossilized remains of dead plants and animals exposed to extreme heat and pressure within the Earth's crust.
    • Examples: Diesel, gasoline, kerosene, LPG, coal tar, naphtha, ethanol.

    C. Gaseous Fuels

    • Definition: Fuels that exist as gases under ordinary conditions.
    • Composition: Composed primarily of hydrocarbons (e.g., methane, propane), hydrogen, carbon monoxide, or mixtures thereof.
    • Advantages: Easily transmitted and distributed via pipes from origin directly to consumption points.
    • Examples: Hydrogen, propane, methane, coal gas, water gas, blast furnace gas, coke oven gas, Compressed Natural Gas (CNG).

    D. Biofuels

    • Definition: Solid, liquid, or gaseous fuels consisting of or derived from renewable biomass.
    • Origin: Produced from rapidly replenishable carbon sources such as plants and plant-derived materials.
    • Examples: Bioethanol, biodiesel, direct biomass fuel.

    E. Fossil Fuels

    • Definition: Hydrocarbons formed over hundreds of millions of years from ancient plant and animal remains subjected to high heat and pressure in the absence of oxygen.
    • Composition: High percentage of carbon content.
    • Examples: Coal, petroleum (liquid petroleum), natural gas.

    F. Nuclear Fuels

    • Definition: Materials used to derive nuclear energy through nuclear reactions .
    • Fissile Nuclear Fuels: \(^{235} \text{U}\) (Uranium-235) and \(^{239} \text{Pu}\) (Plutonium-239), which undergo nuclear fission chain reactions in nuclear reactors.
    • Fusion Fuels: Nuclear fusion serves as the main fuel source for stars (currently not commercially utilized by humans).

    3. Combustion of Fuels

    • Definition: High-temperature exothermic redox chemical reaction between a fuel and an oxidant (typically atmospheric oxygen), yielding oxidized gaseous products (smoke).

    Stoichiometric Combustion Equation for Hydrocarbons

    For a general hydrocarbon \(C_xH_y\):

    \(C_xH_y + zO_2 \rightarrow xCO_2 + \frac{y}{2}H_2O\) where: $$ z = x + \frac{1}{4}y $$

    Example: Burning of Propane (\(C_3H_8\))

    \[ C_3H_8 + 5O_2 \rightarrow 3CO_2 + 4H_2O \]

    4. Calorific Value

    • Calorific Value of Fuel: Total amount of energy released by the complete combustion of a unit mass/volume of fuel.
    • Units: Energy per unit mass/volume (e.g., \(\text{kJ/kg}\) or \(\text{Btu/lb}\)) .
    • Calorific Value of Food: Energy available from food upon digestion (primarily from carbohydrates and fats) .

    Types of Calorific Value

    1. Higher Calorific Value (HCV) / Gross Calorific Value (GCV) / Higher Heating Value (HHV):
    2. Assumes that the water produced during combustion is completely condensed .

    3. The latent heat contained in the water vapor is recovered .

    4. Lower Calorific Value (LCV) / Net Calorific Value (NCV) / Lower Heating Value (LHV):

    5. Products of combustion contain water vapor that is allowed to escape .
    6. The latent heat contained in the water vapor is not recovered [cite: 1].

    5. Gross Combustion Values of Common Materials

    Material Gross Combustion Value (\(\text{Btu/lb}\))
    Carbon 14,093
    Hydrogen 61,095
    Carbon Monoxide 4,347
    Methane 23,875
    Ethane 22,323
    Propane 21,669

    6. Properties of an Ideal Fuel

    An ideal fuel should possess the following characteristics: * High Calorific Value: Yields maximum heat per unit mass [cite: 1]. * Moderate Ignition Temperature: Easily ignited, but not unsafe to store/handle [cite: 1]. * Low Moisture Content: Minimizes energy lost in evaporating water [cite: 1]. * Low Non-Combustible Matter Content: Minimal ash or residue generation [cite: 1]. * Non-Harmful Combustion Products: Environmentally safe, minimal toxic emissions (\(NO_x\), \(SO_x\), etc.) [cite: 1]. * Low Cost: Economically viable [cite: 1]. * Easy and Safe to Transport & Store: Convenient handling logistics [cite: 1].
