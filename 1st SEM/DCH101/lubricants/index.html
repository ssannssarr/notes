    Unit: Lubricants

    1. Definition & Overview

    • Lubricant: A substance (usually organic) introduced to reduce friction between surfaces in mutual contact, thereby reducing the heat generated when surfaces move.

    • Additional functions include transmitting forces, transporting foreign particles, and heating or cooling surfaces.

    • Lubricity: The property of reducing friction.

    • Lubrication: The process or technique of using a lubricant to reduce friction and wear and tear in contact between two surfaces.
    • Applications:
    • Industrial: To ensure proper, efficient functioning of mechanisms and reduce wear.
    • Non-Industrial: Cooking (oils/fats to prevent food sticking), bioapplications (artificial joints), ultrasound examinations, and medical examinations.

    2. Properties of Ideal Lubricants

    A high-quality lubricant generally exhibits the following characteristics:

    • High boiling point and low freezing point (remains liquid over a wide temperature range)
    • High Viscosity Index
    • Thermal stability
    • Hydraulic stability
    • Demulsibility
    • Corrosion prevention
    • High resistance to oxidation

    3. Lubricant Additives

    Modern automotive lubricants contain as many as 10 additives, comprising up to 20% of the overall lubricant composition.

    Additive Primary Function Common Examples / Notes
    Anti-foaming Agents Increase surface tension to prevent foam formation. Silicone compounds
    Viscosity Index Improvers Help lubricants maintain viscosity at high temperatures. Polyacrylates, butadiene
    Antioxidants / Metal Deactivators Suppress oxidative degradation of hydrocarbon molecules. • Free radical inhibitors (e.g., BHT / butylated hydroxytoluene) for low temperatures

    • Dithiophosphates for \(T > 90^{\circ}\text{C}\) where metals catalyze oxidation
    Detergents Keep engine components clean by preventing high-temperature deposit formation. —
    Corrosion Inhibitors Alkaline materials that absorb acids to prevent metal parts from corroding/rusting. Alkylsulfonate salts
    Anti-wear Additives Form protective "tribofilms" on metal parts to suppress wear. Phosphate esters, zinc dithiophosphates

    4. Types of Lubricants

    A. Mineral Oils

    Base oils derived from crude oil, categorized by their prevailing chemical composition:

    • Paraffinic
    • Naphthenic
    • Aromatic

    B. Synthetic Oils

    Synthetic hydrocarbon lubricants produced from petroleum derivatives, including:

    • Polyalpha-olefin (PAO)
    • Synthetic esters
    • Polyalkylene glycols (PAG)
    • Phosphate esters
    • Alkylated naphthalenes (AN)
    • Silicate esters
    • Ionic fluids
    • Multiply alkylated cyclopentanes (MAC)

    C. Solid Lubricants

    • PTFE (Polytetrafluoroethylene): Chemically inert coating layer used in cooking utensils (non-stick) and as an additive in special greases; stable up to \(350^{\circ}\text{C}\).
    • Inorganic Solids: Graphite, hexagonal boron nitride, molybdenum disulfide (\(\text{MoS}_2\)), and tungsten disulfide (\(\text{WS}_2\)).
    • Metals & Alloys: Pure metals, metal alloys, and composites used as grease additives or sole constituents of bearings/sliding surfaces.

    D. Aqueous Lubrication

    • Utilizes liquid-solid interfaces; strongly hydrated brush polymers such as polyethylene glycol (PEG) serve as lubricants in various technological applications.

    E. Biolubricants

    • Derived from vegetable oils and renewable plant/animal sources.
    • Primarily composed of triglyceride esters.

    F. Gas Lubricants

    • Gases like nitrogen and helium are used as lubricants.
    • Key Advantages: Wide operating temperature range, extremely low friction (due to low viscosity), zero vaporization, zero solidification, and no thermal decomposition.

    5. Main Functions of Lubricants

    1. Keep Moving Parts Apart: Creates a physical barrier (thin fluid film) between moving parts to reduce friction, wear, surface fatigue, heat, operating noise, and vibration.
    2. Reduce Friction: Lower lubricant-to-surface friction reduces overall system friction, resulting in decreased wear particles, less heat generation, and improved efficiency.
    3. Heat Transfer: Liquid lubricants absorb and carry away heat efficiently due to their high specific heat capacity through continuous circulation to cooler parts.
    4. Transport Contaminants & Debris: Circulating lubricants carry debris and contaminants to filters. Detergents and dispersants assist in keeping debris suspended for removal.
    5. Transmit Power: Used as hydraulic fluid in hydrostatic power transmission (accounts for a large portion of global lubricant production) and automatic transmission torque converters.
    6. Protect Against Wear: Prevents mechanical wear via physical separation and anti-wear/extreme pressure additives.
    7. Prevent Corrosion: Chemical additives exclude moisture and bond to metallic surfaces, mitigating surface corrosion and rust.
    8. Gas Sealing: Fills microscopic gaps/clearances between moving parts via capillary action (e.g., sealing engine pistons and shafts).# Unit: Lubricants
