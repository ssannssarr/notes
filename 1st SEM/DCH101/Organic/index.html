    Organic Chemistry Notes

    1. Isomerism

    Isomers

    • Compounds having the same molecular formula and same molecular mass, but different arrangements/structures. $$ \boxed{ \text{ Compounds having same moleculer formula but with diff structural formula. } } $$

    Types of Isomerism

    • Structural isomerism
    • Positional isomerism
    • Functional isomerism

    Structural Isomerism

    Example: - Molecular formula: C₄H₁₀ - Different carbon arrangements are possible.

    Positional Isomerism

    Example: - Molecular formula: C₃H₈O - CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–OH → propanol-1 - CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃ → propanol-2

    Functional Isomerism

    Example: - CH₃CH₂OH → ethanol / ethyl alcohol - CH₃–O–CH₃ → ether

    2. Stereoisomers

    Stereoisomers are divided into:

    • Geometrical isomers
    • Optical isomers

    Geometrical Isomerism

    • Occurs in alkenes.
    • Two forms:
    • cis
    • trans

    Examples: - cis-but-2-ene - trans-but-2-ene

    Optical Isomerism

    • Lactic acid is given as an example of a chiral compound.

    3. Valency and Carbon Chains

    • Valency of carbon = 4

    Examples: - CH₄ - C₂H₆ - C₃H₈ - C₄H₁₀ - C₅H₁₂ - C₆H₁₄ - C₇H₁₆ - C₈H₁₈

    Types of Carbon Chains

    1. Straight chains (alkanes)
    2. Branched chains

    4. Nomenclature of Organic Compounds

    • Chemical/systematic names are preferred over common names.
    • School name → systematic name
    • IUPAC = International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry.

    Alkane General Formula

    \[ C_nH_{2n+2} \]

    The first compound in the alkane series is:

    Methane (CH₄)

    5. Cyclic Compounds

    Examples:

    • Cyclopropane → 3-membered carbon ring
    • Cyclopentane → 5-membered carbon ring
    • Cyclohexane → 6-membered carbon ring

    6. Carbocyclic Compounds

    Carbocyclic compounds include:

    (a) Aliphatic

    • Open-chain compounds.

    (b) Aromatic

    Examples:

    • Benzene (C₆H₆)
    • Naphthalene
    • Anthracene

    7. Heterocyclic Compounds

    If an atom such as oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus, or sulphur is present in the ring, it is classified as a heterocyclic compound.

    Examples:

    • Pyridine
    • Pyrrole
    • Furan
    • Thiophene

    8. Classification of Organic Compounds

    Organic compounds are classified based on:

    1. Carbon skeleton
    2. Functional group
    3. Homologous series

    Based on Carbon Skeleton

    • Open-chain / aliphatic
    • Cyclic
    • Carbocyclic
    • Heterocyclic
    • Aliphatic
    • Aromatic

    13. Hydrocarbons

    Hydrocarbons are compounds made mainly from:

    • Carbon (C)
    • Hydrogen (H)

    Main Classes

    1. Alkanes → single bonds
    2. Alkenes → double bonds
    3. Alkynes → triple bonds

    15. Naming Branched Alkanes

    Basic Steps

    1. Find the longest carbon chain.
    2. Number the chain.
    3. Give substituents the appropriate numbers.
    4. Name the substituents.
    5. Combine the locants + substituent name + parent-chain name.

    Examples from the Notes

    • 4-methylhexane
    • 3-methylhexane
    • 2,3-dimethylbutane
    • 3,4-dimethylheptane
    • 3,4,5-trimethylheptane
    • 3,4,5-trimethylhexane

    16. Alkyl Groups

    An alkyl group is formed by removing one hydrogen from an alkane.

    Alkane Alkyl Group
    Methane Methyl → CH₃–
    Ethane Ethyl → C₂H₅–
    Propane Propyl → C₃H₇–
    Pentane Pentyl
    Hexane Hexyl

    General Idea

    Alkane − H → Alkyl group
