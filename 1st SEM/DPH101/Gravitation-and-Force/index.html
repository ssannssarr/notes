    1. Force

    Force is defined as:

    \[ F = ma \]

    where:

    • \(F\) = force
    • \(m\) = mass
    • \(a\) = acceleration

    The SI unit of force is Newton (N).

    Four Fundamental Forces

    1. Gravitational force
    2. Electromagnetic force
    3. Strong nuclear force
    4. Weak nuclear force

    2. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

    Newton's law of gravitation states:

    Every two bodies in the universe attract each other with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

    For two bodies of masses \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), separated by distance \(r\):

    \[ F \propto m_1m_2 \]

    and

    \[ F \propto \frac{1}{r^2} \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \boxed{F = G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}} \]

    where \(G\) is the universal gravitational constant.

    Value of \(G\)

    \[ \boxed{G = 6.674\times10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2}} \]

    \(G\) is universal, meaning its value is the same everywhere.

    3. Gravitational Constant \(G\)

    From

    \[ F = G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2} \]

    we get

    \[ \boxed{G=\frac{Fr^2}{m_1m_2}} \]

    The gravitational constant is defined as the gravitational force between two bodies of unit mass separated by unit distance.

    4. Relation Between \(g\) and \(G\)

    Consider a body of mass \(m\) near the surface of Earth.

    Let:

    • \(M_E\) = mass of Earth
    • \(R_E\) = radius of Earth
    • \(m\) = mass of the body
    • \(g\) = acceleration due to gravity

    The force on the body due to gravity is:

    \[ F_1 = mg \]

    From Newton's law of gravitation:

    \[ F_2 = G\frac{M_E m}{R_E^2} \]

    Since both expressions represent the same gravitational force:

    \[ mg = G\frac{M_E m}{R_E^2} \]

    Cancel \(m\):

    \[ \boxed{g = G\frac{M_E}{R_E^2}} \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \boxed{g=\frac{GM_E}{R_E^2}} \]

    5. Acceleration Due to Gravity

    The acceleration due to gravity is approximately:

    \[ \boxed{g=9.8\ \text{m/s}^2} \]

    Dimensional Formula

    \[ g = \frac{\text{length}}{\text{time}^2} \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \boxed{[g]=[LT^{-2}]} \]

    6. Weight of a Body

    Weight is the gravitational force exerted by Earth on a body.

    \[ \boxed{W=mg} \]

    where:

    • \(W\) = weight
    • \(m\) = mass
    • \(g\) = acceleration due to gravity

    The SI unit of weight is Newton (N).

    Dimensional Formula

    \[ [W]=[MLT^{-2}] \]

    7. Dimensional Formulae

    Gravitational Force

    \[ \boxed{[F]=[MLT^{-2}]} \]

    Gravitational Constant

    From

    \[ G=\frac{Fr^2}{m_1m_2} \]
    \[ [G] = \frac{[MLT^{-2}][L^2]}{[M^2]} \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \boxed{[G]=[M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}]} \]

    Acceleration Due to Gravity

    \[ \boxed{[g]=[LT^{-2}]} \]

    Weight

    \[ \boxed{[W]=[MLT^{-2}]} \]

    Solved Problems

    8. Gravitational Force Between Two Bodies

    Question

    Find the gravitational force between two bodies, each of mass \(20\,\text{kg}\), separated by \(50\,\text{cm}\).

    Given

    \[ m_1=m_2=20\,\text{kg} \]
    \[ r=50\,\text{cm}=0.5\,\text{m} \]
    \[ G=6.67\times10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2} \]

    Using:

    \[ \begin{aligned} F &= G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2} \\ F &= \frac{(6.67\times10^{-11})(20)(20)}{(0.5)^2} \\ F &=(6.67\times10^{-11})(1600) \end{aligned} \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \boxed{F=1.0672\times10^{-7}\ \text{N}} \]

    9. Acceleration Due to Gravity on the Moon

    Question

    What will be the acceleration due to gravity on the Moon if:

    • Radius of Moon = \(\frac14\) radius of Earth
    • Mass of Moon = \(\frac1{80}\) mass of Earth

    Given

    \[ R_M=\frac{R_E}{4} \]
    \[ M_M=\frac{M_E}{80} \]

    We know:

    \[ g=G\frac{M}{R^2} \]

    For Earth:

    \[ g_E=G\frac{M_E}{R_E^2} \]

    For Moon:

    \[ g_M=G\frac{M_M}{R_M^2} \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{g_M}{g_E} &= \frac{M_M}{M_E} \frac{R_E^2}{R_M^2} \end{aligned} \]

    Substituting:

    \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{g_M}{g_E} &= \frac1{80} \left(\frac{R_E}{R_E/4}\right)^2 \\ &= \frac1{80}(4)^2 \\ &= \frac{16}{80} \\ &= \frac15 \end{aligned} \]

    Hence,

    \[ g_M=\frac{g_E}{5} \]

    Taking \(g_E=9.8\,\text{m/s}^2\):

    \[ g_M=\frac{9.8}{5} \]
    \[ \boxed{g_M=1.96\ \text{m/s}^2} \]

    10. Gravitational Force Between the Sun and Jupiter

    Question

    The mass of Jupiter is \(1.9\times10^{27}\,\text{kg}\), the mass of the Sun is \(1.99\times10^{30}\,\text{kg}\), and their mean distance is \(7.8\times10^{11}\,\text{m}\). Calculate the gravitational force exerted by the Sun on Jupiter.

    Given

    \[ m_J=1.9\times10^{27}\,\text{kg} \]
    \[ m_S=1.99\times10^{30}\,\text{kg} \]
    \[ r=7.8\times10^{11}\,\text{m} \]
    \[ G=6.67\times10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2} \]

    Using:

    \[ F=G\frac{m_Jm_S}{r^2} \]
    \[ F= \frac{ (6.67\times10^{-11}) (1.9\times10^{27}) (1.99\times10^{30}) }{ (7.8\times10^{11})^2 } \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \boxed{F\approx4.15\times10^{23}\ \text{N}} \]

    11. Gravitational Force Between an Electron and a Proton

    Question

    Find the gravitational force between an electron and a proton if their separation is \(1\,\text{A}\).

    Given

    Mass of electron:

    \[ m_e=9.1\times10^{-31}\,\text{kg} \]

    Mass of proton:

    \[ m_p=1.67\times10^{-27}\,\text{kg} \]

    Separation:

    \[ r=1\,\text{A} \]

    Since:

    \[ 1\,\text{A}=10^{-10}\,\text{m} \]

    we have:

    \[ r=10^{-10}\,\text{m} \]

    Using:

    \[ F=G\frac{m_em_p}{r^2} \]
    \[ F= \frac{ (6.67\times10^{-11}) (9.1\times10^{-31}) (1.67\times10^{-27}) }{ (10^{-10})^2 } \]

    Therefore,

    \[ \boxed{F\approx1.014\times10^{-47}\ \text{N}} \]
