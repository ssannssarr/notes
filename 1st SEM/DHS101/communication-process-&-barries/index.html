    Communication Process

    The communication process includes 6 main elements, that work together to share information effectively.

    Main Elements

    1. Sender
    2. Message
    3. Channel
    4. Receiver
    5. Feedback
    6. Setting

    1. Sender

    Definition

    The person who starts the conversation to share an idea.

    Description

    The sender creates a thought and adapts it to their tone to the listeners. A good communicator uses a polite, formal tone for elders or respected persons, and an easy, informal tone for friends or juniors.

    Example

    A professor stands in front of the classroom to start a lecture on programming. Here, the professor is the sender.

    2. Message

    Definition

    The actual information, thought, or feeling being shared by the sender.

    Description

    It could be verbal (written or spoken words) or non-verbal (a friendly smile, a polite nod, or maintaining good eye contact). A proper message is always clear, respectful, and gentle.

    Example

    If I write a text like:

    "Respected Sir, could you please guide me on the programming assignment?"

    This polite request is the message.

    3. Channel

    Definition

    The medium or path through which the message is forwarded from the sender to the receiver.

    Description

    It connects the speaker and the listener. It can be a formal channel like an official university email or letter, or an informal channel like a quick chat over social media or a face-to-face conversation.

    Example

    A phone network used for calling a friend, or the air that carries my voice during a face-to-face conversation with my friend.

    4. Receiver

    Definition

    The person who gets the message and reads or listens to it.

    Description

    The receiver breaks down the words to get the meaning. Proper behaviour means the receiver listens or reads patiently without interrupting, showing respect to the sender.

    Example

    A student listening patiently and respectfully while their teacher delivers a lecture.

    5. Feedback

    Definition

    The reply or response sent back to the sender from the receiver.

    Description

    It completely finishes the communication cycle and proves the message was understood. Feedback can be verbal or non-verbal.

    Example

    When my mother explains an important topic to me, I listen carefully and nod my head to show that I've understood it.

    6. Setting

    Definition

    The physical place or situational environment where the communication takes place.

    Description

    The location guides our behaviour. A classroom, library, or office creates a quiet, formal setting that requires strict politeness. A cafeteria, park, or hostel is a relaxed, informal setting.

    Example

    A well-arranged, quiet classroom with student benches, desks facing the teacher's blackboard creates a formal setting for a lecture.

    Communication Barriers

    A communication barrier is any obstacle that disrupts information, preventing the receiver from understanding the sender's intended message.

    Types of Communication Barriers

    1. Language and Semantic Barriers

    Distortions caused by words, signs, symbols, or linguistic differences.

    Lack of Common Language

    Communicating parties speak completely different languages.

    Example:
    An Indian developer discussing code with a non-English-speaking international client.

    Semantics (Word Meaning)

    Words that carry different meanings for different people.

    Example:
    The word "bug" means an insect to a layman, but an error to a computer scientist.

    Vocabulary & Grammar

    Poor sentence structure or punctuation changes the intended message.

    Example:
    Wrongly placed commas in a configuration script that change system execution commands.

    Jargon

    Using specialized, highly technical terms with non-technical people.

    Example:
    Telling a client that your program uses an "asynchronous API microservice" instead of saying it is "fast and reliable."

    2. Physical and Environmental Barriers

    Tangible or situational factors in the surrounding environment that block messages.

    Time Zone & Distance

    Geographic gaps and time-zone differences that delay feedback.

    Example:
    A tech team working in India trying to sync live with a manager in New York.

    Workspace Design and Noise

    Disruptions caused by poor room layout or ambient sound.

    Example:
    Conducting a team meeting next to a loud server room or on a dynamic factory floor.

    3. Socio-Psychological Barriers

    Interferences caused by emotional states, social standing, and identity differences.

    Halo Effect

    A cognitive bias where an overall impression of a person alters how you judge their specific traits.

    Example:
    Thinking a candidate is highly organized and skilled just because they arrive at the interview well-dressed.

    Status Barrier

    Workplace position or authority creates distance and intimidation between employees.

    Example:
    A junior employee or student feeling too anxious to directly report a problem to the President or Principal.

    Different Perceptions of Reality

    Interpreting the exact same situation differently based on your viewpoint.

    Example:
    Two people looking at a multi-coloured object from different angles and disagreeing on its colour due to lighting.

    Distortion, Filtering and Editing

    The systematic alteration of a message as it passes through multiple contact points.

    Example:
    A game of "Chinese Whispers" where the final message is completely different from the original text.

    Past Experience

    Previous life encounters and memories create biases that affect current communication.

    Example:
    A negative past encounter with a stranger ruining someone's communication dynamic when they turn out to be your HOD.

    Poor Listening and Inattentiveness

    Failing to process messages due to internal or external factors.

    • Internal factors: Hunger, fatigue, anger, or family issues.
    • External factors: Zoning out or choosing to ignore the speaker.

    4. Organizational Barriers

    Obstacles tied directly to a company's structure, policy, or hierarchy.

    Key Causes

    • Rigid top-down management structure
    • Complex communication channels
    • Restrictive policies

    Impact

    Distort or block the flow of important workplace information.

    Example:
    Frontline workers cannot answer customer tweets without legal team approval.

    5. Cultural Barriers

    Disruptions caused by geographic roots, regional settings, or background differences.

    Example:
    Misunderstanding caused by varied regional accents or local dialects within the same language.
